Stability Analysis of the Formation Control Scheme

Supplement to the M.Sc. thesis Validación experimental de controladores multiagentes tipo Quad-Rotor (2023)

Julio Cesar Rodríguez-Cervantes

PDFThesis

This supplement was written in 2026 and is not part of the thesis defended in August 2023. It completes the stability argument that the thesis only sketched in Theorem 1, which referred to Ríos et al. [IEEE TIE 2019] for the proof. Equation numbers of the form (T-\(n\)) refer to the thesis. The proofs follow the methodology of Ríos et al. (2019) and of the other works cited. Where a Lyapunov function or a gain condition comes from a reference, it is cited and not re-derived.

1 Scope and corrections

Checking the thesis against the implemented controllers and the model (T-4) turned up the following errata. The corrected forms are the ones used below.

  • (T-8b) should read \(\nu_y=u_m(\cos\phi^\star\sin\theta^\star\sin\psi_d-\sin\phi^\star\cos\psi_d)\). This is the second component of \(g_\xi(\bm\eta)u_m\) and it is consistent with (T-9b).

  • (T-8c) should read \(\nu_z=u_m\cos\phi^\star\cos\theta^\star-g\). This is the third component of \(g_\xi(\bm\eta)u_m-\bm G\) and it is consistent with (T-9a).

  • (T-17) should read \(s=e+k_0\lceil \varepsilon\rfloor^{3/2}\). With weights \((3,2,1)\) for \((e,\varepsilon,v)\), the term \(\lceil \varepsilon\rfloor^{3/2}\) has weight \(3\), equal to that of \(e\). Any other exponent makes \(s\) non-homogeneous. This is also the form implemented in the firmware.

  • (T-14): the implemented STA is \(s=\varepsilon+k_0e\), \(\bar\tau=v-k_1\lceil s\rfloor^{1/2}\), \(\dot v=-k_2\lceil s\rfloor^{0}\), with no \(-k_0\varepsilon\) term in \(\bar\tau\). Then \(\dot s=\bar\tau+k_0\varepsilon\), so \(k_0\varepsilon\) acts as a state-dependent perturbation of the standard STA. This is treated in Proposition 3, and the result is regional, not global.

2 Model, notation and standing assumptions

\(\lceil s\rfloor^{\gamma}:=|s|^\gamma\operatorname{sign}(s)\) and \(\lceil s\rfloor^{0}:=\operatorname{sign}(s)\). Classes \(\mathcal K\), \(\mathcal{KL}\) and \(\mathcal{KL}_T\) (the last vanishing in finite time) and the properties US, UES, UFTS, GUFTS, ISS and FT-ISS are used as in Sontag (1989) and Bernuau et al. (2014). The agent index \(i\) is dropped when no confusion arises. Agent \(n\) is the leader and \(j=1,\dots,n-1\) are the followers. The graph is a star centred at the leader. Each agent obeys (T-4): \[\label{eq:model}\tag{1} \begin{aligned} \dot{\bm\xi}_1&=\bm\xi_2, & \dot{\bm\xi}_2&=g_\xi(\bm\eta_1)u_m-\bm G-\Lambda_\xi\bm\xi_2+\bm d_\xi(t),\\ \dot{\bm\eta}_1&=\bm\eta_2, & \dot{\bm\eta}_2&=J\bm\tau+\Xi\bm w_\eta(\bm\eta_2)-\Lambda_\eta\bm\eta_2+\bm d_\eta(t), \end{aligned}\] with \(\bm\xi_1=(x,y,z)\), \(\bm\eta_1=(\phi,\theta,\psi)\), \(\bm G=(0,0,g)\), \(u_m=u/m\), and \[\label{eq:gxi}\tag{2} g_\xi(\bm\eta_1)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\phi\sin\theta\cos\psi+\sin\phi\sin\psi\\ \cos\phi\sin\theta\sin\psi-\sin\phi\cos\psi\\ \cos\phi\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.\] Let \(\hat{\bm\xi}_1,\hat{\bm\xi}_2,\hat{\bm\xi}_3,\hat{\bm\eta}_1,\hat{\bm\eta}_2,\hat{\bm\eta}_3\) be the states of the FT-SMO (T-5). The offsets \(C_{n,j}\) are constant. Define \(\bm\xi_{dj}:=\bm\xi_{1n}+C_{n,j}\) for followers and \(\bm\xi_{dn}:=\bm\xi_d\) for the leader. Define \(\psi_{dj}:=\psi_n\) and \(\psi_{dn}:=\psi_d\). The errors are \[\label{eq:errors}\tag{3} \bm e_\xi=\bm\xi_1-\bm\xi_d,\quad \bm\varepsilon_\xi=\hat{\bm\xi}_2-\dot{\bm\xi}_d,\quad \bm e_\eta=\bm\eta_1-\bm\eta_d,\quad \bm\varepsilon_\eta=\hat{\bm\eta}_2-\dot{\bm\eta}_d,\] with \(\bm\eta_d=(\phi^\star,\theta^\star,\psi_d)\). For a follower \(\dot{\bm\xi}_d=\bm\xi_{2n}\). The virtual control is \(\bm w_\xi:=g_\xi(\bm\eta_1)u_m-\bm G-\bm\nu\). The references are \[\label{eq:refs}\tag{4} \begin{aligned} u_m&=\sqrt{\nu_x^2+\nu_y^2+(\nu_z+g)^2}, & \phi^\star&=\arcsin\!\big[u_m^{-1}(\nu_x\sin\psi_d-\nu_y\cos\psi_d)\big],\\ \theta^\star&=\arctan\!\big[(\nu_z+g)^{-1}(\nu_x\cos\psi_d+\nu_y\sin\psi_d)\big]. \end{aligned}\] The control (T-10) is \[\label{eq:ctrl}\tag{5} \bm\nu=\bar{\bm\nu}+\Lambda_\xi\bm\xi_2+\ddot{\bm\xi}_d-\hat{\bm\xi}_3,\qquad \bm\tau=J^{-1}\big(\bar{\bm\tau}-\Xi\bm w_\eta+\Lambda_\eta\bm\eta_2+\ddot{\bm\eta}_d-\hat{\bm\eta}_3\big),\] where \(\bar\nu_q=k_{0q}\!\int e_q+k_{1q}e_q+k_{2q}\varepsilon_q\) for \(q=x,y,z\) (T-11), and each companion matrix \[\label{eq:A}\tag{6} A_q=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\k_{0q}&k_{1q}&k_{2q}\end{pmatrix}\] is Hurwitz (T-12). The attitude controllers \(\bar\tau_l\), \(l=\phi,\theta,\psi\), are (a) PID (T-13), (b) STA (T-14, as implemented), (c) continuous twisting (T-15), (d) STSMC (T-16) and (e) NTSMC (T-17, corrected).

Assumption 1. Thesis Assumptions 3–4: the desired trajectories are bounded and \(C^\infty\), and the disturbances \(\bm d_\xi,\bm d_\eta\) are uniformly bounded and Lipschitz with known bounds.

Assumption 2. \(\nu_{z}+g>0\) along solutions (for instance by saturating \(\bar\nu_z\)), so \(\theta^\star\) is well defined and \(u_m>0\). The angle \(\phi^\star\) is always well defined, because by Cauchy–Schwarz \((\nu_x\sin\psi_d-\nu_y\cos\psi_d)^2\le(\nu_x^2+\nu_y^2)(\sin^2\psi_d+\cos^2\psi_d)\le u_m^2\).

Assumption 3. The thrust is bounded: \(u_{mi}\le\bar u\).

Assumption 4. The feed-forward signals \(\ddot{\bm\xi}_{di}\) (for followers, the leader acceleration, transmitted or estimated) and \(\ddot{\bm\eta}_{di}\) are available and bounded.

Assumption 5. Solutions exist on \([0,T_0]\) (no finite escape).

Justification of A5 (sketch). On \([0,T_0]\) the cancellation terms in (5) that involve \(\bm\xi_2,\bm\eta_2\) use the measured states, so the residual forcing consists of disturbances and estimation errors. These are bounded by A1 and Lemma 2. The position loop is \(\ddot{\bm\xi}=g_\xi u_m-\bm G-\Lambda_\xi\dot{\bm\xi}+\bm d_\xi\), a linear system driven by a bounded input (A3), so it cannot escape in finite time. In every attitude controller \(\bar\tau\) has at most linear growth in \((e,\varepsilon,v)\), because the fractional powers are sublinear and \(\dot v\) is linear or bounded. A system with linearly bounded right-hand side and bounded forcing has no finite escape. This is the standard argument of Ríos et al. (2019). It is kept as an assumption because (T-5) is not reproduced here.

Lemma 1 (reference attitude). Under A2, (4) gives \(\phi^\star\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\), \(\theta^\star\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\), and \(g_\xi(\bm\eta_d)u_m-\bm G=\bm\nu\) with \(\bm\eta_d=(\phi^\star,\theta^\star,\psi_d)\).

Proof. Put \(a=\nu_x\cos\psi_d+\nu_y\sin\psi_d\), \(b=\nu_z+g>0\) and \(c=\nu_x\sin\psi_d-\nu_y\cos\psi_d\), so \(u_m^2=a^2+b^2+c^2\). Then \(\sin\phi^\star=c/u_m\) and, since \(\cos\phi^\star\ge0\), \(\cos\phi^\star=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}/u_m\). Also \(\tan\theta^\star=a/b\) with \(\cos\theta^\star>0\) gives \(\cos\theta^\star=b/\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) and \(\sin\theta^\star=a/\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\). Hence \(u_m\cos\phi^\star\cos\theta^\star=b\) and \(u_m\cos\phi^\star\sin\theta^\star=a\). From (2), the first component of \(g_\xi(\bm\eta_d)u_m\) is \[u_m\cos\phi^\star\sin\theta^\star\cos\psi_d+u_m\sin\phi^\star\sin\psi_d=a\cos\psi_d+c\sin\psi_d=\nu_x,\] and the second is \(a\sin\psi_d-c\cos\psi_d=\nu_y\). The third equals \(b=\nu_z+g\). ◻

3 Observer and closed loop for \(t\ge T_0\)

Lemma 2 (FT-SMO, cited). Under A1 there exist gains of the third-order observer (T-5), with injections \(\lceil \cdot\rfloor^{2/3},\lceil \cdot\rfloor^{1/3},\lceil \cdot\rfloor^{0}\) chosen from the Lipschitz bounds of \(\bm d_\xi,\bm d_\eta\), such that the estimation error is UFTS. Hence there is a finite \(T_0\), bounded for bounded initial estimation errors, with \(\hat{\bm\xi}_1=\bm\xi_1\), \(\hat{\bm\xi}_2=\bm\xi_2\), \(\hat{\bm\xi}_3=\bm d_\xi\), \(\hat{\bm\eta}_1=\bm\eta_1\), \(\hat{\bm\eta}_2=\bm\eta_2\), \(\hat{\bm\eta}_3=\bm d_\eta\) for \(t\ge T_0\) (T-6).

This is proved in Ríos et al. (2017) (see also Levant, 2003) and is not repeated here. The error dynamics are driven only by the derivative of the disturbance, which is bounded by A1.

For \(t\ge T_0\) we have \(\bm\varepsilon_\xi=\bm\xi_2-\dot{\bm\xi}_d\) and, by the definition of \(\bm w_\xi\), \(g_\xi u_m-\bm G=\bm\nu+\bm w_\xi\). Substituting (5) into the error dynamics (T-7) gives \[\label{eq:cl}\tag{7} \dot{\bm e}_\xi=\bm\varepsilon_\xi,\ \ \dot{\bm\varepsilon}_\xi=\bar{\bm\nu}+\bm w_\xi,\qquad \dot{\bm e}_\eta=\bm\varepsilon_\eta,\ \ \dot{\bm\varepsilon}_\eta=\bar{\bm\tau}.\] Indeed \(\dot{\bm\varepsilon}_\xi=\bm\nu+\bm w_\xi-\Lambda_\xi\bm\xi_2+\bm d_\xi-\ddot{\bm\xi}_d=\bar{\bm\nu}+\bm w_\xi+(\bm d_\xi-\hat{\bm\xi}_3)\), and \(\dot{\bm\varepsilon}_\eta=\bar{\bm\tau}+(\bm d_\eta-\hat{\bm\eta}_3)\) after the terms \(\Xi\bm w_\eta\), \(\Lambda_\eta\bm\eta_2\), \(\ddot{\bm\eta}_d\) cancel. The last brackets vanish for \(t\ge T_0\). Note that the \((\bm e_\eta,\bm\varepsilon_\eta)\) subsystem does not depend on the position states. This relies on the exact use of \(\ddot{\bm\eta}_d\) in (5) (A4), and it is what makes the scheme a cascade.

Lemma 3 (coupling bound). Under A2–A3, \(\bm w_\xi=\big(g_\xi(\bm\eta_1)-g_\xi(\bm\eta_d)\big)u_m\) and \(\lVert \bm w_\xi\rVert\le L_g\bar u\,\lVert \bm e_\eta\rVert\) with \(L_g=6\).

Proof. By Lemma 1, \(\bm\nu=g_\xi(\bm\eta_d)u_m-\bm G\), which gives the identity. Every partial derivative of a component of \(g_\xi\) is a sum of at most two products of sines and cosines (for example \(\partial_\phi g_{\xi,1}=-\sin\phi\sin\theta\cos\psi+\cos\phi\sin\psi\) and \(\partial_\theta g_{\xi,1}=\cos\phi\cos\theta\cos\psi\)), so its magnitude is at most \(2\). Hence \(\lVert \partial g_\xi/\partial\bm\eta\rVert_2\le\lVert \partial g_\xi/\partial\bm\eta\rVert_F\le\sqrt{9\cdot4}=6\). Since \(\mathbb{R}^3\) is convex, \(g_\xi(\bm\eta_1)-g_\xi(\bm\eta_d)=\int_0^1\frac{\partial g_\xi}{\partial\bm\eta}(\bm\eta_d+s\bm e_\eta)\,ds\;\bm e_\eta\), and \(u_m\le\bar u\) completes the proof. The constant \(6\) is conservative. ◻

4 Position loop

Proposition 1 (ISS w.r.t. \(\bm e_\eta\)). Let \(t_0\ge T_0\) and \(q\in\{x,y,z\}\). With \(\chi_q=(\int e_q,\,e_q,\,\varepsilon_q)^\top\) and \(B=(0,0,1)^\top\), the closed loop (7) gives \(\dot\chi_q=A_q\chi_q+B\,w_q\), where \(w_q\) is the \(q\)-th component of \(\bm w_\xi\). There exist \(\kappa\ge1\) and \(\lambda>0\), depending only on \(A_q\), such that for \(t\ge t_0\) \[\label{eq:iss}\tag{8} \lVert \chi_q(t)\rVert\le\kappa e^{-\lambda(t-t_0)}\lVert \chi_q(t_0)\rVert+\frac{\kappa}{\lambda}L_g\bar u\,\lVert \bm e_\eta\rVert_\infty ,\] with \(\lVert \bm e_\eta\rVert_\infty=\sup_{[t_0,t]}\lVert \bm e_\eta\rVert\). If \(\bm e_\eta\equiv0\) on \([t_1,\infty)\), then \(\lVert \chi_q(t)\rVert\le\kappa e^{-\lambda(t-t_1)}\lVert \chi_q(t_1)\rVert\), i.e. the loop is UES.

Proof. From \(\frac{d}{dt}\!\int e_q=e_q\), \(\dot e_q=\varepsilon_q\) and \(\dot\varepsilon_q=\bar\nu_q+w_q=k_{0q}\!\int e_q+k_{1q}e_q+k_{2q}\varepsilon_q+w_q\) we get \(\dot\chi_q=A_q\chi_q+Bw_q\). Since \(A_q\) is Hurwitz, \(\lVert e^{A_qt}\rVert\le\kappa e^{-\lambda t}\) for some \(\kappa\ge1\), \(\lambda>0\) (Khalil, 2002). Variation of constants gives \[\chi_q(t)=e^{A_q(t-t_0)}\chi_q(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^te^{A_q(t-s)}Bw_q(s)\,ds .\] Since \(\lVert B\rVert=1\), \(|w_q|\le\lVert \bm w_\xi\rVert\le L_g\bar u\lVert \bm e_\eta\rVert\) by Lemma 3, and \(\int_{t_0}^te^{-\lambda(t-s)}ds\le1/\lambda\), estimate (8) follows. If \(\bm e_\eta\equiv0\) on \([t_1,\infty)\), then \(w_q\equiv0\) there and the first term of the variation-of-constants formula alone remains. ◻

5 Attitude loop

For a vector field \(F\) and weights \(r=(r_1,\dots,r_m)>0\), homogeneity of degree \(q\) means \[F_k(\lambda^{r_1}x_1,\dots,\lambda^{r_m}x_m)=\lambda^{r_k+q}F_k(x)\quad\text{for all }\lambda>0.\] Discontinuous systems are understood in the sense of Filippov, with \(\lceil s\rfloor^{0}\) replaced by the set-valued \(\operatorname{sign}\). Two facts from Bernuau et al. (2014) are used. (H1) A homogeneous system of negative degree that is globally asymptotically stable is GUFTS. (H2) For \(\dot x=F(x,w)\) homogeneous of degree \(q\) with weights \(r\) for \(x\) and \(\tilde r\) for \(w\), global asymptotic stability at \(w=0\) and \(\min\tilde r>0\) imply ISS, and if moreover \(q<0\) the system is FT-ISS.

Write \(e,\varepsilon,\tau\) for one channel \(l\), and \(v\) for the state of the controller. For \(t\ge T_0\) the channel satisfies \(\dot e=\varepsilon\), \(\dot\varepsilon=\bar\tau\) by (7).

Proposition 2 (attitude loop, controllers a, c, d, e). Let \(t_0\ge T_0\) and assume the gains satisfy the conditions of the cited references.

  1. PID: with \(\chi=(\int e,e,\varepsilon)\), \(\dot\chi=A\chi\) and \(\lVert \chi(t)\rVert\le\kappa e^{-\lambda(t-t_0)}\lVert \chi(t_0)\rVert\) (GUES).

  2. TC: \(x=(e,\varepsilon,v)=0\) is GUFTS, with settling time \(T\le5V(x_0)^{1/5}/\kappa_V\).

  3. STSMC: \(x=0\) is GUFTS, with settling time \(T\le4V(x_0)^{1/4}/\kappa_V\).

  4. NTSMC: \(x=0\) is GUFTS.

  5. For c–e, with \(\dot\varepsilon=\bar\tau+\rho(t)\) and \(\rho\) measurable and essentially bounded (in particular Lipschitz), the system is FT-ISS w.r.t. \(\rho\).

Proof. a) \(\dot\chi=A\chi\) with \(A\) Hurwitz (T-13), so \(\lVert \chi(t)\rVert=\lVert e^{A(t-t_0)}\chi(t_0)\rVert\le\kappa e^{-\lambda(t-t_0)}\lVert \chi(t_0)\rVert\) for all \(\chi(t_0)\).

c) The closed loop is \[\dot e=\varepsilon,\quad \dot\varepsilon=v-k_0\lceil e\rfloor^{1/3}-k_1\lceil \varepsilon\rfloor^{1/2},\quad \dot v\in-k_2\operatorname{sign}(e)-k_3\operatorname{sign}(\varepsilon).\] Take weights \((3,2,1)\) for \((e,\varepsilon,v)\) and degree \(q=-1\). Then \(\dot e=\varepsilon\) scales as \(\lambda^{2}=\lambda^{3+q}\), and in \(\dot\varepsilon\) the terms \(v\), \(\lceil e\rfloor^{1/3}\), \(\lceil \varepsilon\rfloor^{1/2}\) scale as \(\lambda^1=\lambda^{2+q}\). The sign terms in \(\dot v\) scale as \(\lambda^0=\lambda^{1+q}\). So the system is homogeneous of degree \(-1\). For gains satisfying the conditions of Torres-González et al. (2017), see also Ríos et al. (2019), a positive definite, radially unbounded Lyapunov function \[V=\beta_1|e|^{5/3}+\beta_2e\varepsilon+\beta_3|\varepsilon|^{5/2}+\beta_4e\lceil v\rfloor^{2}-\beta_5\varepsilon v^3+\beta_6|v|^5,\qquad\beta_i>0,\] homogeneous of degree \(5\), is constructed there with \(\dot V\le-\kappa_VV^{4/5}\) along solutions. This gives GUFTS. Moreover \(\frac{d}{dt}V^{1/5}=\frac15V^{-4/5}\dot V\le-\kappa_V/5\) while \(V>0\), so \(V\) vanishes no later than \(T\le5V(x_0)^{1/5}/\kappa_V\).

d) In the coordinates \(x=(e,\varepsilon,v)\) with \(s=\varepsilon+k_0\lceil e\rfloor^{2/3}\) the closed loop is \[\dot e=\varepsilon,\quad\dot\varepsilon=v-k_1\lceil s\rfloor^{1/2},\quad\dot v\in-k_2\operatorname{sign}(s).\] The variable \(s\) has weight \(2\), since \(\varepsilon\) has weight \(2\) and \(\lceil e\rfloor^{2/3}\) has weight \(3\cdot\frac23=2\). Hence \(\lceil s\rfloor^{1/2}\) has weight \(1=2+q\) and the sign term has weight \(0=1+q\). The system is homogeneous of degree \(-1\) with weights \((3,2,1)\). The expression for \(\dot s\) is singular at \(e=0\), but the analysis is done in \((e,\varepsilon,v)\) where no singularity appears. For gains satisfying the conditions of Fridman et al. (2015), see also Ríos et al. (2019), the quadratic form \(V=\zeta^\top P\zeta\) with \(P=P^\top>0\) and \(\zeta=(\lceil e\rfloor^{2/3},s,\lceil v\rfloor^{2})\), whose entries all have weight \(2\), so \(V\) has degree \(4\), satisfies \(\dot V\le-\kappa_VV^{3/4}\). As in c), this gives GUFTS and \(T\le4V(x_0)^{1/4}/\kappa_V\).

e) With the corrected (T-17), \(s=e+k_0\lceil \varepsilon\rfloor^{3/2}\), the closed loop is \[\dot e=\varepsilon,\quad\dot\varepsilon=v-k_1\lceil s\rfloor^{1/3},\quad\dot v\in-k_2\operatorname{sign}(s).\] For weights \((3,2,1)\) the term \(\lceil \varepsilon\rfloor^{3/2}\) has weight \(2\cdot\frac32=3\), equal to the weight of \(e\), so \(s\) is homogeneous of weight \(3\). Then \(\lceil s\rfloor^{1/3}\) has weight \(1=2+q\), \(\dot e=\varepsilon\) has weight \(2=3+q\), and the sign term has weight \(0=1+q\). The system is homogeneous of degree \(-1\). For gains satisfying the conditions of Kamal et al. (2016), a weighted Lyapunov function is given there, and with (H1) it proves GUFTS. The function is not reproduced here.

f) The perturbation \(\rho\) enters the \(\varepsilon\)-equation, whose weighted scaling is \(\lambda^{2+q}=\lambda^1\). Assigning \(\rho\) the weight \(\tilde r=1\), the perturbed system \(F(x,\rho)\) is homogeneous of degree \(-1\) in the sense of (H2). Since \(\min\tilde r=1>0\), \(q<0\), and the unperturbed system is GAS by c)–e), (H2) gives FT-ISS: there exist \(\beta\in\mathcal{KL}_T\) and \(\gamma\in\mathcal K\) with \(\lVert x(t)\rVert\le\beta(\lVert x(t_0)\rVert,t-t_0)+\gamma(\lVert \rho\rVert_\infty)\). ◻

Remark 1. In c)–f), a bounded compensation residual, for example \(\rho=\bm d_\eta-\hat{\bm\eta}_3\) on \([0,T_0]\) (bounded by Lemma 2), leaves the attitude state bounded by \(\gamma(\lVert \rho\rVert_\infty)\) plus a decaying term. Perturbations that enter through the \(v\)-channel have weight \(0\) and are not covered by (H2). They are handled in the cited works through gain conditions for Lipschitz perturbations, which are not restated here.

Proposition 3 (STA as implemented). Take controller b): \(s=\varepsilon+k_0e\), \(\bar\tau=v-k_1\lceil s\rfloor^{1/2}\), \(\dot v=-k_2\lceil s\rfloor^{0}\), with \(k_0,k_1,k_2>0\). Let \(\bar v:=v+k_0\varepsilon\). For \(t\ge T_0\), \[\dot s=-k_1\lceil s\rfloor^{1/2}+\bar v,\qquad \dot{\bar v}=-k_2\lceil s\rfloor^{0}+k_0\bar\tau .\] Let \(K\subset\mathbb{R}^3\) be compact and let \(\varrho_K:=k_0\max_{(e,\varepsilon,v)\in K}|v-k_1\lceil \varepsilon+k_0e\rfloor^{1/2}|\). Suppose \(k_1,k_2\) satisfy the gain conditions of Moreno and Osorio (2012) and Seeber and Horn (2017) for a perturbation of the integral channel bounded by \(\varrho_K\) (in particular \(k_2>\varrho_K\)). Then every solution that stays in \(K\) for \(t\ge T_0\) reaches \(s=\bar v=0\) in finite time \(T_s\), and afterwards \((e,\varepsilon,v)\to0\) exponentially.

Proof. From \(\dot e=\varepsilon\) and \(\dot\varepsilon=\bar\tau\) we get \(\dot s=\bar\tau+k_0\varepsilon=-k_1\lceil s\rfloor^{1/2}+v+k_0\varepsilon\), and \(\dot{\bar v}=\dot v+k_0\dot\varepsilon=-k_2\lceil s\rfloor^{0}+k_0\bar\tau\). Set \(\delta(t):=k_0\bar\tau(t)\) along the solution. Since the solution stays in \(K\), \(|\delta(t)|\le\varrho_K\). The pair \((s,\bar v)\) is therefore a super-twisting algorithm whose second equation carries a measurable perturbation bounded by \(\varrho_K\) (equivalently, a perturbation with Lipschitz constant \(\varrho_K\) in the first equation). The cited gain conditions are sufficient for finite-time convergence of \((s,\bar v)\) to \(0\) for every such perturbation. This gives \(T_s<\infty\) with \(s=\bar v=0\) for \(t\ge T_s\). There, \(v=-k_0\varepsilon\) and \(\bar\tau=v-k_1\lceil 0\rfloor^{1/2}=-k_0\varepsilon\), so \(\dot\varepsilon=-k_0\varepsilon\). Also \(s=0\) gives \(\varepsilon=-k_0e\) and hence \(\dot e=-k_0e\). Therefore \(e,\varepsilon,v\) decay as \(e^{-k_0(t-T_s)}\). The sliding dynamics are UES, and \(|k_0\bar\tau|=k_0^2|\varepsilon|\) decreases, so the bound by \(\varrho_K\) remains valid. ◻

Remark 2. Proposition 3 is a regional (semi-global) result, and it is conditional on the solution staying in \(K\). The gains must be raised with the size of \(K\) through \(\varrho_K\). Adding the missing term, \(\bar\tau=v-k_1\lceil s\rfloor^{1/2}-k_0\varepsilon\), gives \(\dot s=-k_1\lceil s\rfloor^{1/2}+v\) and \(\dot v=-k_2\lceil s\rfloor^{0}\). This is the standard unperturbed STA, which is globally finite-time stable for \(k_1,k_2>0\) (Moreno and Osorio, 2012). Then \(s=0\) gives \(\dot e=-k_0e\), and the result becomes global. The thesis (T-14) as implemented lacks this term.

6 Main result

Theorem 1 (restated and corrected). Under A1–A5, with the controls (T-9)–(T-11), (4), (5) and any attitude controller a)–e) (for b) under the hypothesis of Proposition 3), the errors \((\bm e_\xi,\bm\varepsilon_\xi,\bm e_\eta,\bm\varepsilon_\eta)\) of every agent converge to zero. The position errors converge exponentially. The attitude errors converge in finite time for c–e and exponentially for a, b. Consequently the followers reach the pattern \(\bm\xi_{1j}-\bm\xi_{1n}=C_{n,j}\) and the leader tracks \(\bm\xi_d\).

Proof. (i) Exact compensation. By Lemma 2, for \(t\ge T_0\) the loop is (7). By A5 the state at \(T_0\) is finite. The attitude subsystem \((\bm e_\eta,\bm\varepsilon_\eta)\) does not depend on the position states (Section 3).

(ii) Cascade. For c–e, Proposition 2 gives a finite \(T_1\ge T_0\), depending on the state at \(T_0\), with \(\bm e_\eta=\bm\varepsilon_\eta\equiv0\) for \(t\ge T_1\). By Lemma 3, \(\bm w_\xi\equiv0\) on \([T_1,\infty)\). By (8), \(\chi_q(T_1)\) is bounded, so \(\lVert \chi_q(t)\rVert\le\kappa e^{-\lambda(t-T_1)}\lVert \chi_q(T_1)\rVert\) (UES). For a, b, there are \(c,\mu>0\) with \(\lVert \bm e_\eta(t)\rVert\le ce^{-\mu(t-T_0)}\) (Proposition 2a), and for b Proposition 3, where the finite-time phase before \(T_s\) only adds a bounded transient). Inserting this in the variation-of-constants formula of Proposition 1, \[\lVert \chi_q(t)\rVert\le\kappa e^{-\lambda(t-T_0)}\lVert \chi_q(T_0)\rVert+\kappa L_g\bar u\,c\!\int_{T_0}^te^{-\lambda(t-s)}e^{-\mu(s-T_0)}ds ,\] and \(\int_0^\tau e^{-\lambda(\tau-s)}e^{-\mu s}ds\le\tau e^{-\min(\lambda,\mu)\tau}\le(e\delta)^{-1}e^{-(\min(\lambda,\mu)-\delta)\tau}\) for any \(\delta\in(0,\min(\lambda,\mu))\). Hence \(\chi_q\to0\) exponentially. The same conclusion follows from the ISS cascade lemma (Sontag, 1989; Khalil, 2002, Lemma 4.7). So \(\bm e_\xi,\bm\varepsilon_\xi\to0\) for each agent, and \(\bm\varepsilon_\eta\to0\) is part of the attitude result.

(iii) Formation. For a follower \(j\), \(\bm e_{\xi j}=\bm\xi_{1j}-\bm\xi_{1n}-C_{n,j}\to0\) gives the geometric pattern, and for the leader \(\bm e_{\xi n}\to0\) gives \(\bm\xi_{1n}\to\bm\xi_d\). In the star graph each follower depends only on the leader, through the bounded signals \(\bm\xi_{1n},\bm\xi_{2n},\ddot{\bm\xi}_n,\psi_n\) (A4), and the leader does not depend on any follower. With the exact feed-forward (5) the error system of each agent is the one analysed in Propositions 1–3, which does not contain the states of other agents. There are therefore no interconnection loops, and the convergence of the whole formation follows agent by agent. ◻

7 What is not proven

The following are outside this analysis: the discrete-time implementation, communication delays and packet loss (Crazyradio), actuator saturation beyond A3 (including the effect of saturating \(\bar\nu_z\) on the linear position analysis), and the approximation of \(\ddot{\bm\eta}_d\). For the last, only ISS-type ultimate bounds follow from Proposition 2f) when the residual is bounded. A4 assumes the boundedness of the signals \(\ddot{\bm\eta}_d\) along the closed loop, and this is not proven. The gain conditions for c–e are taken from the cited works and are not checked for the gains used in the experiments.
